Motion Studio / Drill 15

Form and edges

The motion from drill 14, unchanged. Only the drawing is different: the left is outlines filled in, the right is drawn as things with volume, standing in light.

Why the first drawings looked flat

Every shape was a silhouette: one even outline, closed all the way round, filled with one ink, with straight parallel hatching clipped to its edge. That is how a cut-out looks. Close reading of Kevin T. Ngo’s dew (the dewdrop on the leaf at 25 s, the orange and the fly at the start) shows the opposite in every case: marks wrap around forms, outlines change weight and break, and the drop shows the room inside it.

What each switch is

  • Cross-contour strokes. Marks follow the surface instead of a ruler: along the veins of the leaf, around the ellipse of the pool. A stroke that bends with the form tells the eye the form bends. In dew, the orange carries meridian lines and short strokes that wrap around it.
  • Lost and found edges. Where a surface turns toward the light the outline thins and breaks; where it turns away the line thickens. One rule, light from the upper left, decides every edge in the picture, so the shapes agree about where the light is.
  • Drop as a lens. A transparent thing is drawn by what it does to what is behind it. The drop shows the pool at its top and the leaf at its bottom, upside down, darkens at its thick rim, and gathers light on the side away from the sun. Volume comes from optics, not from shading.
  • Contact shadow. The falling drop throws a shadow on the water that tightens and darkens as it nears. Without it a falling thing floats in front of the background instead of moving through the space.
  • Light beams. Pale diagonal bands, lightening whatever they cross. Light becomes a layer that every object sits inside, which flattens nothing and unifies everything.
  • Construction lines. Thin blue guides left in: the drop’s path, the water line, the direction of the light. They make the picture feel drawn and measured, as in dew, where drafting lines run straight through the drop.

The lines that matter

// Edge weight from the light: thick where the surface turns away, gone where it faces it
const lit = normal.x * light.x + normal.y * light.y;
if (lit > lost) skip(); else width = base * (0.35 + (0.55 - lit) * 0.9);

// Cross-contour: short streamlines along a direction field instead of clipped parallel lines
flowStrokes(ctx, leaf, { dir: (x, y) => axis + side(x, y) * 0.55, tone });

// Lens: draw the world again inside the drop, scaled and turned upside down
ctx.clip(drop); ctx.scale(-k, -k); drawPool(); drawLeaf();
