## E. Film devices (pictures and story, not claims)
- The two travellers, the hill country, the narrow plank bridge, the river, the stone house: the film's setting. Hegel gives none.
- The bridge standoff ("neither steps aside") as the occasion of the struggle; the drop below as "staking one's life". The fight as four clear key poses.
- The pear and the stream drink for desire (¶175); the cup and bread for the lord's enjoyment (¶190).
- The lord seeing a hat and not a face (¶191 to ¶192).
- Both evenly matched over the rail; the straw hat looking at the water, letting go of the man and grabbing the rope rail, as clinging to life (¶189 to ¶190).
- The red coat's pack (carried from scene 2) handed over and its strap across the bondsman's chest, and the rope darkening, for "his chain" (¶190).
- Water carried for the lord to drink, for the bondsman working on the thing while the lord enjoys it (¶190).
- The clay jar (Hegel names no material and no object beyond "the thing", *das Ding*), the four parallel horizontal throwing rings and his fingers' fit in them for formative activity made permanent (¶195 to ¶196); faint pencil onion-skin hand traces kept from revision 2.
- Equal height and build for both travellers (¶186, ¶189).
- Gendered "he" follows the masculine noun *der Knecht* and the translations; Hegel's subject is "consciousness" (*das Bewußtsein*, neuter) and "self-consciousness".

## F. Could not verify
- Pinkard's wording outside ¶195 to ¶196 (his old site is gone and the book was not reachable). His ¶195 to ¶196 phrases come from a secondary excerpt.
- Mueller 1958, Kelly 1966 and McDowell 2003 were not read; all three are known through SEP, Cambridge Core listings or descriptions.
- Hegel's lecture use of the triad for Kant (*LHP* III: 477), known only through SEP.
- Rubin's 1915 date (revision 2 only; no longer used).
- Kojève page numbers (pp. 6 to 7 for "pure prestige") are inferred from page markers in the OCR text, not from a page image. The critic's claim that "counts as nothing" echoes Kojève p. 19 was not checked; the phrase was replaced anyway on textual grounds (¶192).
- Whether "a kind of story" is fair to Hegel's own presentation is a matter of reading, not of fact (section D, historical or conceptual).
- The IEP reading that the bondsman submits "out of fear of death" (row N18) is a commentator's ordering; Hegel's text does not narrate the surrender as caused by fear, it places fear of death in the bondsman's experience (¶194).
- Not used, flagged for a future film: Susan Buck-Morss, "Hegel and Haiti" (2000), argues that the Haitian Revolution shaped this section. Contested; not checked.

## G. Do not copy from
- The marxists.org (Baillie) page: every heading inside IV A is the translator's, not Hegel's. That covers "1. Duplicated Self-Consciousness", "2. The Conflict of Self-Consciousness in Self-opposition", "3. Lord and Bondsman", "(a) Lordship", "(b) Fear" and "(c) The Formative Process of Self-Enfranchisement". The only heading in Hegel's German here is "A. Selbstständigkeit und Unselbstständigkeit des Selbstbewußtseins; Herrschaft und Knechtschaft".
- The archive.org Miller scan: numbered paragraphs of Findlay's analysis are printed alongside Hegel's text and look like Hegel at a glance.
- Popular "master-slave dialectic" summaries that end with the slave overthrowing the master, or with a "synthesis", describe Kojève, Marx or the triad legend, not chapter IV A.
