## C. "Thesis, antithesis, synthesis" (researched, not used on screen)

| Claim | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Hegel did not adopt the thesis, antithesis, synthesis language for his own philosophy | SEP Maybee, "Hegel's Dialectics", section 3, citing Mueller 1958: 411 to 412 and Solomon 1983: 23 | checked against SEP |
| The language is Fichte's (and Kant's antinomies are set out as thesis and antithesis) | SEP Maybee, section 3 (Hegel "never adopted Fichte's triadic" thesis, antithesis, synthesis "language"; Kant's antinomies "consist of a thesis and antithesis"); Findlay 1958, pp. 69 to 70: the terms "are in fact not frequently used by Hegel: they are much more characteristic of Fichte" | checked |
| Hegel apparently used the three words once, in lectures, to describe Kant's philosophy | SEP Maybee, citing *Lectures on the History of Philosophy* III: 477 | checked against SEP; the lecture text itself not read |
| Hegel criticised formulaic use of the triadic form ("triplicity") as reducing it to a "lifeless schema" | German Preface ¶50: "zum leblosen Schema ... und die wissenschaftliche Organisation zur Tabelle herabgebracht" | checked. Nuance: Hegel attacks the *formalistic* use while praising Kant's triplicity raised to its "absolute meaning". Hegel's work is full of triads; the myth is the formula and its labels, not the threeness. |
| Chalybäus presented Hegel's logic as a series of theses, antitheses and syntheses | Chalybäus 1837, around pp. 299 to 301: Becoming is "die Synthesis der beiden ersten"; "So viel über die erste und schwierigste These, Antithese und Synthese"; the pattern recurs "in allen folgenden Thesen, Antithesen und Synthesen" | checked in the 1837 first edition scan |
| Classic statement of the "legend" | Gustav E. Mueller, "The Hegel Legend of 'Thesis-Antithesis-Synthesis'", *Journal of the History of Ideas* 19 (1958): 411 to 414 | citation checked; article not read |
| The triad reading still has defenders in some form | SEP Maybee cites Forster 1993, Stewart 2000 and Fritzman 2014 as giving versions of it currency | checked against SEP. "Myth" is fair for "Hegel's method is thesis-antithesis-synthesis", not for "Hegel never thought in threes". |

## E. Film devices (pictures, not claims)
- The faces-or-vase figure (Edgar Rubin, 1915) as Hegel's middle term. Not Hegel's image. Rubin's date is from general knowledge, not checked against a primary source.
- Blue construction lines for the pure notion of recognition (beat 2) and for a face not yet drawn (beat 9).
- Fruit and branch for the object of desire; a plate of fruit for the lord's enjoyment; a clay vessel for the formed thing (Hegel names no material); a halftone echo for the bondsman's recognition of the lord; a tether for "his chain"; a yellow ghost outline for the lord's command; onion-skin throwing rings for formative activity made permanent.
- Mirror-identical profiles for A and B (a geometric need, see script.md). It also fits ¶186: at first they are simply "one individual ... confronted by another individual" (Miller).
- Gendered "he" for the bondsman follows the masculine noun *der Knecht* and the translations; Hegel's subject is "consciousness" (*das Bewußtsein*, neuter).

## F. Could not verify
- Pinkard's wording outside ¶195 to ¶196 (his old site is gone and the book was not reachable). His ¶195 to ¶196 phrases come from a secondary excerpt.
- Mueller 1958, Kelly 1966 and McDowell 2003 were not read; all three are known through SEP, Cambridge Core listings or descriptions.
- Hegel's lecture use of the triad for Kant (*LHP* III: 477), known only through SEP.
- Rubin's 1915 date.
- Not used, flagged for a future film: Susan Buck-Morss, "Hegel and Haiti" (2000), argues that the Haitian Revolution shaped this section. Contested; not checked.

## G. Do not copy from
- The marxists.org (Baillie) page: every heading inside IV A is the translator's, not Hegel's. That covers "1. Duplicated Self-Consciousness", "2. The Conflict of Self-Consciousness in Self-opposition", "3. Lord and Bondsman", "(a) Lordship", "(b) Fear" and "(c) The Formative Process of Self-Enfranchisement". The only heading in Hegel's German here is "A. Selbstständigkeit und Unselbstständigkeit des Selbstbewußtseins; Herrschaft und Knechtschaft".
- The archive.org Miller scan: numbered paragraphs of Findlay's analysis are printed alongside Hegel's text and look like Hegel at a glance.
- Popular "master-slave dialectic" summaries that end with the slave overthrowing the master, or with a "synthesis", describe Kojève, Marx or the triad legend, not chapter IV A.
