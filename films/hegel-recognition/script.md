# Lord and Bondsman: script (revision 3.1, narrated parable)

Film on Hegel, *Phenomenology of Spirit* (1807), chapter IV A, "Lordship and Bondage" (*Herrschaft und Knechtschaft*), paragraphs 178 to 196 in A. V. Miller's numbering, with 174 to 177 as the run-up and 197 as the step after. Square, 1080, 12 drawings a second, 120 seconds, 13 scenes, calm voice-over. Every claim is keyed to sources.md (section B, rows N1 to N30).

Revision 3 (2026-11-07) rewrote revision 2, a wordless chain of symbols, as a story about two people carried by narration, after Henry watched the first 38 seconds of revision 2 and found it made no sense. Revision 2 is kept as `script-v2-symbolic.md`. Revision 3.1 (the same day) applies an independent critic's review; the changes are listed at the end.

## Header

- **Idea, in one sentence.** You become sure of yourself only through being recognised by another self; the man who wins that recognition by force gets it from someone with no choice, which leaves him no surer of himself, while the man forced to work for him finds himself in the lasting thing he makes, though he is still a bondsman.
- **The viewer's question at the start.** What do we want from other people, and can you get it by winning?
- **The turn.** The winner gets exactly what he fought for, and it leaves him no surer of himself. The loser, made to work, picks up the jar he made and his fingers fit the rings his own hands left in it.
- **The takeaway.** Respect only counts when it comes from someone free to refuse it. And even work done under orders can hold a mind of your own. (The first sentence is the film's application of Hegel, marked in the narration by "Perhaps that is the lesson". The second is close to ¶196.)

## The world and the two characters

A dry hill country in late summer, drawn on kraft paper in ink and loose watercolor. A river runs through a gorge, crossed by a narrow plank footbridge with rope rails. On the far bank, a small stone house with a porch and a clay bank by the water. Even daylight throughout; no heat haze, sweat or golden glow to tell the viewer whom to pity.

- **The red-coat traveller** (becomes the lord). Long rust-red coat (risograph red), bare head, a walking staff, a travelling pack on his back from scene 2 on.
- **The straw-hat traveller** (becomes the bondsman). Wide straw hat (a flat ochre ellipse, readable at any size), ochre tunic, a small bundle on his back. The hat stays on for the whole film: it is his silhouette.
- **Same height, same build, same stride.** Nothing in their bodies says which one will rule. Lordship comes out of the fight, not out of nature (¶186, ¶189). Tell them apart by color and silhouette (staff and bare head against hat brim), never by size.
- Faces in profile drawn as simple shapes. No names, no dialogue. Only the narrator speaks. Neither man is drawn as cruel or noble; the lord looks content, the bondsman looks ordinary.

**Text on screen.** The full narration goes on a separate caption track (a WebVTT file shipped with the MP4), not burned in. Burned in are only the title, the end card and three quotation cards from Hegel, each under 12 words and on screen at least 3 seconds, in EB Garamond italic:
- Scene 3: "It exists only in being acknowledged." Hegel, ¶178 (8 words)
- Scene 10: "Work ... is desire held in check." Hegel, ¶195 (8 words)
- Scene 11: "He acquires a mind of his own." Hegel, ¶196 (9 words)

## Story spine

- **Once upon a time** there were two travellers who took what they wanted from the world, a pear here, a drink there.
- **Every day** the wanting came back, and nothing they took could look back at them.
- **Until one day** they met in the middle of a narrow bridge, and each saw the other as nothing more than a body in the way.
- **Because of that,** each risked his life to prove he was more than a body: a free self.
- **Because of that,** with both of them over the rail, one let go of the fight and clung to life instead.
- **Because of that,** the other became his lord and got the bowing he fought for, from a man with no choice; and the one who clung became his bondsman, carrying the lord's pack and water and making what he was told to make.
- **Until finally** the bondsman picked up the jar he had made and his fingers fit the rings his own hands had left in it: he had found a mind of his own, though he was still a bondsman.

## Story notes (why it is built this way)

- **Cause and effect a newcomer can follow.** Each scene answers "why did that happen?" from the one before: the wanting comes back, so they need someone who can look back; they see each other only as bodies, so neither steps aside; to prove they are more than bodies, they risk their lives; a dead man recognises no one, so the fight cannot end in a death; one clings to life, so he serves; the lord's victory leaves him no surer of himself; the bondsman's work lasts. Each beat is an ordinary situation (eating, a standoff on a bridge, a fight, holding on, carrying someone's bag, serving a meal, making a pot), not a symbol to decode.
- **The motive is Hegel's, not Kojève's.** The fight is not for passage or prestige. Each sees the other "like ordinary objects ... submerged in the being of Life" (¶186) and must show that he himself "is not attached to life" (¶187). Scene 4 shows the first, scene 5 says the second.
- **The surrender comes from clinging to life, not from losing.** Scene 6 freezes them evenly matched, both over the rail. The difference is where they look: the red coat at the man, the straw hat at the water. His hand then lets go of the man and grabs the rail. That is ¶189 to ¶190 as a gesture.
- **The protagonist is the bondsman.** He is the one who changes. The film opens on the two as equals and ends on his jar.
- **One visual metaphor, set up before it pays off.** The four throwing rings are planted in scene 10 (a close-up the audience sees being made) and paid off in scene 11 (his fingers fit them). The pack strap is the only standing image of bondage, and it is a literal strap whose handover (scene 8) has continuity from scene 2.
- **The fleeting and the lasting sit side by side.** In scene 11 the lord's cup is empty and the jar is still there: desire's "fleeting" satisfaction against work as "fleetingness staved off" (¶195).
- **No moral tilt.** The film does not judge a cruel lord against a noble worker. The lord is content and is given no feelings Hegel does not give him; the bondsman suffers no extra cruelty; the click is shot in the same plain light as everything else. The reversal itself (¶192 to ¶193) stays.
- **The question returns.** Scene 1 asks what we want from each other; scene 13 answers it over the whole road the story travelled.

## Accuracy guard rails for the animator

- The bondsman is still a bondsman at the end. He answers the summons (scene 12). No escape, no uprising, no lord left starving or alone. That ending would be Kojève's or Marx's reading, not Hegel's text (sources.md, section D).
- The lord's enjoyment works. He is content (¶190). What fails is the worth of the recognition (¶191 to ¶192), not his dinner. No sulking or brooding face: Hegel gives the lord no feelings.
- The two travellers are equals at the start and evenly matched in the fight: same size, same build.
- The bondsman gives in because he clings to life (he looks at the water and grabs the rail), not because he is losing.
- The jar is ordered by the lord (scene 10 starts with the lord pointing). The work is service.
- Say and write "lord" and "bondsman", never "master" and "slave". Hegel's word is *Knecht*. The narration glosses "bondsman" once ("bound to the other, now his lord").
- Mutual recognition does not happen in this film. Do not show the two reconciling.
- Do not put any heading from the marxists.org (Baillie) page on screen; they are the translator's, not Hegel's.
- Every detail of the setting (travellers, bridge, pear, pack, jar, house) is the film's. The narration says so in scene 1.

## Revision 3.1: what changed, per scene

- **Sc 1.** "Hegel answered with a kind of story. We've only added faces." became "Hegel answered with a bare story. Every detail here is ours." (the film adds far more than faces).
- **Sc 2.** "For that you need another self." became "Only another self can." The returning want is shown by body pose (reach, hand to stomach), not a glance. The red coat carries a pack from here on.
- **Sc 3.** "and not of the other" became "but only in his own eyes" (¶186: self-certainty "still has no truth"). Quotation card added.
- **Sc 4.** "Someone must step back. But stepping back means admitting the other counts for more." (Kojève's fight for pure prestige, and ¶185's outcome used as a cause) became "Neither steps aside. To each, the other is just a body in the way." (¶186). One second added.
- **Sc 5.** "So each tries to prove he is free, that he does not cling to life, by risking it." became "To prove he is more than a body, a free self, each risks his life." (¶187). The grapple is staged as four clear key poses.
- **Sc 6.** The freeze is now even: both over the rail; the red coat looks at the man, the straw hat at the water.
- **Sc 7.** "One flinches ... One is now lord; the other, bondsman." became "One clings to life, and life becomes his chain. He will serve as bondsman, bound to the other, now his lord." (no "One ... One"; one gloss of "bondsman"). His hand lets go of the man and grabs the rail. The hat stays on. The pack handover moves to scene 8.
- **Sc 8.** "But he counts that someone as nothing" became "But a bow from a man with no choice leaves him no surer of himself." (¶192, the bower's dependence). The pack handover opens the scene and motivates the "days later" cut. The lord's flat face and the "plate and cup stay full, he refills them" business are cut; he looks content, and only the eyeline (a hat, not a face) remains.
- **Sc 9.** The trip, the cracked jug, the sweat and the rule against drinking are cut. Narration: "Fear made him give in, and fear keeps him working. He carries water for someone else to drink."
- **Sc 10.** Opens with the lord pointing at the clay bank, then his cup. The grooves are now four parallel horizontal throwing rings. Quotation card added.
- **Sc 11.** No golden glow or sunlit grooves; plain light, faint pencil traces. The bondsman no longer straightens under the strap. Quotation card added.
- **Sc 12.** "and Hegel leaves him there" became "What he has gained is inward." (¶193, ¶197). Shot from outside: a dark doorway and a hand.
- **Sc 13.** "respect you force from someone tells you little. It counts from someone free to refuse." became "respect only counts when it comes from someone free to refuse it."
- **Text on screen.** The full narration moves to a caption track; only three Hegel quotations, the title and the end card are burned in.
