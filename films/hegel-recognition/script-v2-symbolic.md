# Lord and Bondsman: script

Film on Hegel, *Phenomenology of Spirit* (1807), chapter IV A, "Independence and dependence of self-consciousness: lordship and bondage" (*Herrschaft und Knechtschaft*), paragraphs 178 to 196 in A. V. Miller's numbering, with 175 to 177 as the run-up. Square, 1080, 12 drawings a second, 86 seconds, 9 beats, no narration. Every claim is keyed to sources.md.

Revision 2 (2026-11-06), after the script critic's review. Changes are listed at the end.

## The idea, in one sentence
You only become a self by being recognised by another self that you recognise in return: the lord who forces recognition gets it only from someone he counts as nothing, and the bondsman, made to work for him, finds his own mind in the lasting form his work gives a thing.

## The misconception the viewer arrives with
Power is winning. The one on top gets what he wanted, and a self is something you have alone, inside your head. (A second, common misconception about Hegel in general, "thesis, antithesis, synthesis", is deliberately left out of the film; see the note at the end.)

## The click, as something that moves on screen
The bondsman lifts the vessel he made under orders. As its edge locks against his face and fits it, the throwing rings left in the clay by his own hands light up: his movement, kept in the thing. The vessel's outline is the shape that first appeared between the two faces at the start. He sees himself in work he did for another, and he is still tethered.

## Why it matters now
Approval you have forced, bought or engineered tells you little, because it comes from someone you made unable to refuse it; recognition counts when it comes from someone free to withhold it. And people working under constraint can still find a mind of their own in work done well. (Both are the film's application, not Hegel's text.)

## Beats

Layout throughout: two profiles facing each other across the frame, left (A, who becomes the lord) and right (B, who becomes the bondsman). Their front edges bound the empty middle of the paper. When both stand level, that middle reads as a vessel: the faces-or-vase figure.

**Geometry rule for the animator.** A thrown vessel is mirror-symmetric, so A's and B's profiles must be exact mirror images of each other from beat 2 on. Otherwise the vase between them in beat 2 and the vessel in beats 7 to 9 are different shapes and the rhyme breaks. Build one profile curve and mirror it; tell A and B apart only by pose, rendering and color (A gets riso yellow accents, B gets ink and ochre).

If the film runs long, trim the struggle (beat 3) by 2 seconds and the fear beat (beat 6) by 1 second. Do not trim the hold in beat 4 or the vase reading in beat 2.

### Why caption 2 says "is a self", not "exists"
Miller has "it exists only in being acknowledged" (¶178), and the German is "es ist nur als ein Anerkanntes" ("it is only as something recognised"). On screen, "exists" invites the wrong reading that a person physically depends on being recognised. What is at stake is being a self, existing "in and for itself" as self-consciousness. "A self is a self only when recognised by another" keeps Hegel's "for another" (*für ein Anderes*), keeps the "only", and says what is at stake. It is 10 words.

## The visual through-line, and why this one
One shape carries the whole film: the empty space between two facing profiles, which reads as a vessel (the faces-or-vase figure, Edgar Rubin, 1915; a modern device, not Hegel's).

- It makes Hegel's claim visible without words. A profile only exists against a ground; the vessel only exists while two faces bound it. Hegel calls the relation the middle term (¶184, ¶188), and later calls work itself "this negative middle term" (¶195). When one party dies, "the middle term collapses into a lifeless unity" (¶188). Beat 4 shows exactly that.
- It joins the two halves of the section. Recognition (beats 2 to 5) happens in the middle space. Work (beats 7 and 8) produces a real vessel of the same shape. That link, "by shaping the vessel he shapes the space where recognition happens", is the film's reading, not Hegel's, and is labelled as such in sources.md. The click itself rests on what Hegel does say: the formative activity becomes the lasting form of the thing, and in it the worker sees his own independence (¶195). That is why the throwing rings light up at the click; the profile fit is the rhyme.
- The blue construction lines carry the ideal: the pure notion of recognition in beat 2, the face that is still only planned in beat 9. The film opens and closes on the same unfinished plan.
- It plays to what the studio does well: two things that respond to each other (mirrored motion with lag, lunge and recoil, one line fading as its partner fades, two edges locking into one), a bend with the tip lagging (the bow), lost and found edges (the lord's fraying profile), loose pigment that runs and settles (fear becoming clay), cross-contour strokes wrapping a turning form, onion skin as kept movement, and construction lines as the drawing of something not yet real.
- A reflection in water was considered for the click and rejected: any puddle shows you your face, so it would say "he looked in a mirror", not "he found himself in what he made".

## Accuracy guard rails for the animator
- The film stops where Hegel's section stops. The bondsman gains "a mind of his own" but is still a bondsman (the tether stays on through beat 9). Hegel does not say the bondsman overthrows the lord, that history belongs to the worker, or that the two reach mutual recognition here. Those are Kojève's and Marx's readings, or later stages of the book; see sources.md.
- The lord is recognised (¶191), and his enjoyment works (¶190). What fails is the worth of the recognition, not its arrival. No empty plate, no starving lord: that is a Kojève or Marx style reversal.
- Beat 9's blue face never inks and is never summoned by the vessel. It was planned in beat 2 and is still only planned.
- The work is commanded: the vessel's first outline is the lord's yellow, not B's ink.
- "Lord and bondsman", not "master and slave". Hegel's word is *Knecht* (servant, bondsman), not *Sklave*.
- Do not put any heading from the marxists.org (Baillie) page on screen as Hegel's. Within this chapter the only heading in Hegel's German is "A. Selbstständigkeit und Unselbstständigkeit des Selbstbewußtseins; Herrschaft und Knechtschaft". "1. Duplicated Self-Consciousness", "2. The Conflict of Self-Consciousness in Self-opposition", "3. Lord and Bondsman", "(a) Lordship", "(b) Fear" and "(c) The Formative Process of Self-Enfranchisement" are all Baillie's.

## Note on "thesis, antithesis, synthesis"
Checked and not used on screen. The claim holds up (sources.md, section C). Hegel did not describe his own method with these three words. They belong to Fichte (and Kant's antinomies are set out as thesis and antithesis), and Chalybäus's 1837 exposition presented Hegel's logic as a chain of theses, antitheses and syntheses. It is left out for two reasons. First, in 86 seconds a debunking hook competes with the one idea, and a newcomer would leave with "Hegel didn't say that" instead of "a self needs recognition". Second, the film's central image is a shape in the middle of two others, which a viewer primed with "synthesis" would read as a compromise between lord and bondsman. That is exactly the wrong reading. The section's real movement is a reversal from within: lordship shows "that its essential nature is the reverse of what it wants to be", and servitude "will really turn into the opposite of what it immediately is" (¶193, Miller; German *das Verkehrte*, *sich umkehren*).

Optional end card for a separate short on Hegel's method, not this film: "Not thesis, antithesis, synthesis. A reversal from within." (8 words)
